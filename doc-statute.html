Chapter I · Articles 1–8

General provisions

The statute regulates the name, seat and legal status of the association; its objectives and activities; the conditions and manner of membership; the rights, obligations and responsibilities of members; organisation and management; representation; financing; property; the public nature of its work; the adoption and amendment of the statute; the termination of the association; and all other matters of importance to its operation.

The association is a voluntary, independent, non-partisan, non-governmental and non-profit association of citizens, established in accordance with the Constitution of the Republic of North Macedonia and the Law on Associations and Foundations. It acquires the status of a legal entity on the day of its entry into the Central Register.

Its work rests on the principles of legality, voluntary participation, equality, non-discrimination, transparency, publicity, professionalism, independence, responsibility and respect for human rights.

The association may open regional offices, representative offices, branch units, information centres, project offices and innovation and educational centres in the country and abroad, and may join national and international associations, federations and networks. A decision to change the seat is taken by the Assembly.

The logo, emblem, graphic mark and corporate visual identity are the intellectual property of the association. No natural or legal person may use, modify or reproduce the logo without the written consent of the Managing Board. Standards for the logo, colours, typography and promotional materials are set out in a separate rulebook.

The chapter also establishes the association's digital identity: an official website and domain, electronic mail, cloud information systems, digital platforms, mobile applications, social media profiles and electronic document management systems. It sets up a central digital archive for statutes, minutes, decisions, financial documentation, contracts, projects, publications and correspondence, and recognises qualified electronic signatures, electronic seals and time stamps, so that electronically signed documents count as official documents of the association. Sessions of the bodies may be held physically, online or in hybrid form.

Chapter III · Articles 21–30

Objectives and activities

Mission. To contribute to the development of digital society, innovation, education, science and sustainable development through the application of modern information technologies, international cooperation, research, education and the development of human capacity. The association works in the public interest and promotes equality, inclusiveness, transparency, digital ethics and European values.

Core objectives include developing digital literacy and digital competences, supporting education at all levels, encouraging scientific research, advancing information technology and artificial intelligence, developing STEM education, robotics and cyber security, driving digital transformation and the digital economy, supporting innovation and entrepreneurship, improving digital public services, promoting smart cities and smart villages, protecting the environment through digital technology, narrowing the digital divide, creating equal opportunities for all groups, developing international cooperation and contributing to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

Specific objectives address digital inclusion of young people, women, persons with disabilities and older people; rural development; support for the start-up ecosystem; research centres, innovation laboratories and technology parks; digital entrepreneurship and remote work; digital health; and the digitisation and protection of cultural heritage.

Activities include training, conferences, seminars, workshops, summer and winter schools, competitions and olympiads, fairs and exhibitions, public debates, campaigns, hackathons, innovation forums, academies, courses, online training, webinars, study visits, youth conferences and lifelong learning programmes. The association may also develop curricula, teaching materials, digital textbooks and e-learning platforms, carry out research, publish scientific papers, prepare analyses, studies, strategies, policies and methodologies, conduct evaluations and provide consultancy — as well as develop websites, mobile applications, information systems, software, cloud platforms, data analysis systems, artificial intelligence and machine learning systems and blockchain solutions.

Chapter IV · Articles 31–44

Membership

Membership is voluntary and open to any domestic or foreign natural or legal person who accepts the objectives, mission, statute and ethical principles of the association. No one may be discriminated against on grounds of nationality, ethnicity, language, religion, sex, age, political conviction, disability, social status or any other ground established by law.

The categories of membership are: founders, regular members, youth members, student members, volunteers, expert members, institutional (legal entity) members, honorary members and international members. Regular membership is open to adults who submit an application, accept the mission, respect the statute and the code of ethics, and are admitted by decision of the Managing Board. Youth membership covers ages 15 to 29; student membership is for students enrolled in higher education institutions.

Expert members may include university professors, teachers, researchers, engineers, physicians, lawyers, economists, ICT experts, scientists and consultants, and may be appointed to expert commissions, advisory bodies and project teams. Legal entities — companies, institutions and organisations — may join as institutional members and participate through an authorised representative.

Chapter V · Articles 45–60

Bodies of the association

The bodies are the Assembly, the Managing Board, the President, the Vice-President, the Secretary General, the Supervisory Board, the Scientific and Advisory Council, the Council for Projects and International Cooperation, and the Commission for Digital Ethics and Artificial Intelligence.

The Assembly is the highest body and consists of all regular members, each with one vote. It adopts and amends the statute, approves the mission, vision and strategic documents, elects and dismisses the President and the members of the Managing and Supervisory Boards, adopts annual reports and financial accounts, decides on the membership fee, on establishing legal entities, on joining networks, on status changes and on the dissolution of the association. It meets in regular session at least once a year.

The Managing Board is the executive body, with 5 to 11 members elected by the Assembly for a four-year mandate and eligible for re-election. Selection takes account of gender equality, professional diversity, breadth of expertise, transparency and integrity. It implements Assembly policy, proposes strategic documents, adopts annual operating plans and internal rulebooks, manages projects and property, admits members, appoints project teams, forms commissions and proposes the budget.

The President is the legal representative, elected for four years; the Vice-President deputises in their absence; the Secretary General is the professional executive head of administration. The Supervisory Board has three members who may not simultaneously sit on the Managing Board, and supervises legality, finances and the use of funds.

Decisions are taken by a majority of the members present, except that amendments to the statute, status changes and dissolution require a two-thirds majority of those present.

Chapter XI
